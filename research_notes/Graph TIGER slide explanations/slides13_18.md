## Proposed explanations

**Slide13 — Stage2 overview.** This slide separates three questions: can we reproduce TIGER, does graph input help, and does better text encoding help? On the same smaller Beauty dataset, our RPG-style model rises from0.0725 to0.0767 with the graph. Think725 versus767 correct top-ten hits per10,000 users. The updated Qwen values are0.0739 and0.0775; published-paper results remain references because some implementation and embedding choices differ.

**Slide14 — Reproduction check.** We changed the item codes to test whether RQ-VAE alone explains our TIGER shortfall. Random, hashing and RQ-VAE codes all remain below their paper references; for example0.0546/0.0642≈85%. That suggests a broader reproduction issue, without identifying its cause. Residual k-means reaches0.0583—about7% better than our RQ-VAE—and avoids autoencoder training, although its cluster centers still need fitting.

**Slide15 — Most reliable result.** Different seeds rerun training with different randomness. The gate improved Recall in all six matched runs:0.0725→0.0767, or(0.0767−0.0725)/0.0725≈5.8%. That means about42 extra hits per10,000 users. The paired test's reported p=.0014 supports consistency under its assumptions, not certainty. The gate adds about2% recommender parameters, excluding the separately trained graph model's cost.

**Slide16 — Bigger text encoder.** Here Qwen3 replaces the model that turns each product description into a vector; it does not replace the recommender. Better product descriptions might improve the later codes. Updated results are0.0739 for8B without graph and0.0775 for4B with gate. The latter is only0.0008 above gated Sentence-T5, and the available seeds do not establish a reliable extra gain. The visible chart numbers are older.

**Slide17 — Conclusion.** Our best-supported finding is about where to add the graph: keep text-derived codes and let a learned input gate use collaborative information. This helped across six seeds; changing the codes gave less consistent results. Bigger text encoders and our single-seed ACERec-inspired tests did not establish reliable additional Recall gains. Next, compare more matched runs, measure total cost, and investigate products with little training history.

**Slide18 — Closing.** Close with the project question and the supported answer: can an explicit purchase graph help a generative recommender? In our tested setup, yes—adding graph vectors through an input gate improved the matched baseline. A moisturizer's description explains what it is; shared shoppers add another clue. Invite questions about the mechanism, fair comparisons, and the remaining reproduction limits.
